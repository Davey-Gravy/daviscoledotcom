## Where it started
Based on what you've seen on this blog, I tend to like doing hard things - randomly flashing Android ROMs at 2am, 400m intervals, engineering graduate school, etc. I thought this was a general trait about myself until April 2025. To set the scene, I'm 5 months into working for the data center consultancy. The nature of this business is seasonal and it's a lull at the time, so I'd spend a couple hours at the gym 3-4 times a week. I knew I was frustrated with how difficult it is to overcome inertia in someone's lifestyle business. Development is outsourced to India for the same expense as my salary, and I was not satisfied with their output. My boss tells me about how we want to grow and get our name out there more, but he doesn't seem to really want it like that.

For some reason I wanted to do Romanian deadlfits to work on my glutes and hamstrings. I tend to channel my frustration and stress into the activities I lose myself in (sports, music, video games, etc.), so I was lifting heavy, 135 lbs. On my last rep of my last set of 10, I rounded my back at the bottom and felt a different kind of jolt through my lower back. 

Yup, I'd done it. 

I spent the next 10 minutes keeled over not knowing what to do, then I called my dad to get picked up.

This is the beginning of a saga that continues to this day involving stress, anxiety, myofunctional habits, and an identity crisis.

## April
Immediately after hurting myself

I spent the year coming apart in a specific order. Back first. Then the jaw — TMD. Then the rest of me compensating for both: the right side of my neck and shoulder, my right arm, my abs, the right groin and leg and foot. My tongue sits wrong now; when I put it where it belongs I feel it in my feet and my hips.

<!--more-->

I didn't slow down for any of it. That's not a brag and it isn't a complaint. It's just what happened, and it's the most honest thing I can tell you about my first year.

I knew what a PhD was. What I didn't plan for was how cleanly it would fit into a life that was already full — TA'ing, research, coursework, and data center consulting on the side, all of it last fall, none of it displacing anything. It stacked. There was never a moment the PhD *started*, because I never cleared room for it to start in. I absorbed it the way I absorb most things: by deciding I could.

On paper it worked. Good marks, research moved, TA of the year for my department. Here's the part the paper leaves out: I did all of it while my body was rerouting an injury through every joint it had, and I delivered anyway. I'm not asking for credit for that. I'm pointing at it because it's the whole pattern in one sentence.

My strategy for everything is to try harder. Grit my teeth and push. It's more or less who I am, and most of the time it works — it worked on the courses, it worked on the consulting. It is also, fairly literally, why my jaw is like this. A year of clenching, which I treated by clenching harder. I'm not going to pretend the strategy is wrong; it got me here. I'm learning, slowly, where it stops working.

I put in two fellowship applications in the fall, NDSEG and GRFP. NDSEG went in. GRFP I missed — I misread the deadline. I was at my desk on the day I thought was the day, ready to submit. Ten minutes later I was on the phone with my advisor, certain I'd thrown away something I couldn't get back. I hadn't. You make the call, take the hit, file the next thing. But it goes on the record that I sat in that chair and felt the floor go, because pretending I didn't would be the dishonest version.

The thing I'm most disappointed by isn't the body or the deadline. I thought a university would come with people. Community. The social thing universities are nominally for. It mostly didn't, and Worcester isn't going to make up the difference — there's food and there's home, and that's the menu. I'd have liked it otherwise. It wasn't. I made the year work without it, which I'm a little proud of and a little unsettled by.

Nobody warns you that adulthood is mostly reassembly. The routines that held you up as a kid were built on someone else's schedule. You rebuild them by hand, alone, while everything is already running. I'm bad at it. I'm getting less bad at it. That's the real progress bar this year, and it isn't the one on my CV.

Money's fine, for the record. The market's been on a tear since April, the stipend covers what I need, and I started investing early enough that it isn't a worry. Open a brokerage and a Roth IRA as soon as you can. That one I got right and I'm not going to be modest about it.

What I haven't gotten right is the question under all of it: whether this is mine, or whether it's ego. I'm so smart, I have to prove I'm so smart, please hire me. You can't out-work that question, which is a problem, because out-working things is the only tool I fully trust.

April forced it. A long-term relationship ended. I'm not writing that part out here — not because I can't, but because some restraint is its own kind of honesty.

I don't have a resolution and I'm not going to manufacture one so this ends cleanly. The point was never that I figured it out. The point is that my entire instinct is to grit through a year like this and never say a word, and this time I'm saying the word.

That's the part that took something. Not the courses. This.

So: year one. I carried more than I should have and most of it landed. My jaw is the receipt. I still don't know whether the PhD is the thing I want or the thing I'm proving — and I'm choosing to keep working while I hold that question open, instead of letting it pick the answer for me.

I'll take that over certainty.
